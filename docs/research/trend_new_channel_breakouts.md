## 1. Patterns (from 14 channels with a 100k+ Short)
- **Shorts posted before the first 100k Short:** median about 2.5, range 0 to 38. In 10 of 14 it came within the first 7 Shorts.
- **Did the breakout video differ?** In 10 of 14 the format was set from video 1 and copied a format already working elsewhere ("Ranking…", AI kid or vegetable comedy, "The last is…"). All 4 late breakouts (StreamVault, Ramya, CtrlPlusF, plus Evelyn's 11M jump) came right after a **topic or format change**, usually to an already popular format. None came from posting more of an unchanged, weak format.
- **Early low-view videos lifted later?** No: Golazo's first 8 and animalultraai's videos 15–40 stayed low. OverseerOS cannot show later lifts either (REPORTED, overseeros.com/blog/youtube-breakout-videos, 2026-08-15).
- **Decay after the breakout:** median views fell after the first weeks in 6 of 7 channels with enough data (PK 12.6k → 2.1k; Catkin 2.25M → 262k).
- **Views do not equal subscribers:** compilations and AI slop convert badly (Ramya 215 subs after 259k views; ifmancer 2.35K after 5M).
- **Cadence:** median about 1 Short per day, but CrackWise (1 per week) and Goorfo (9 Shorts in total) show it is not required.

## 2. Large-sample benchmarks
- **vidIQ "First 10 videos" (REPORTED, vidiq.com/research/youtube-first-10-videos-study, 2026-07-30):** 367k Shorts channels created in H1 2025. Median 30-day views: 1,362 on the 1st upload, 1,344 on the 10th, so **flat**. The median best-of-first-10 was 8,832, and 48% reached 10k. Only channels that kept posting are counted (survivorship).
- **OverseerOS (REPORTED, 2026-10-31):** Shorts from channels with 100–999 subscribers had a median of 1,141 views; 0.4% of under-1k-subscriber Shorts passed 100k.
- **YouTube's recommendations team (REPORTED, SEJ, 2020, old):** a channel is not demoted for a few weak videos, and a new channel does not help.
- **Counter-cases (REPORTED, 2024, old):** 90 near-identical daily AI Shorts got 15–100 views each, then about 0 (nikolay100.com). One Short got 300 views on YouTube and 17.5k on TikTok (godo.substack.com).

## 3. What this means for us (65 Shorts, median 1.6k, best 8.2k)
Our median is typical for a new channel, but **our best across 65 Shorts is below the median channel's best across just 10**. Medians stay flat over time, and no channel I measured broke out by repetition alone.
- **Keep the channel and change the format: most consistent (medium confidence).** Every late breakout came on an existing channel after a format or topic change, and old videos carry no measured penalty.
- **Start a new channel: neutral.** New channels hit early, but that is survivorship plus launching with an already proven format; no fresh-channel boost is shown. It costs almost nothing (69 subs), so do it only if the new format's audience doesn't match comics.
- **Change niche: weak support.** Narrated formats broke out in history (ifmancer), Minecraft (Catkin) and facts (CrackWise), so format and packaging seem to matter more than niche (low confidence).

## 4. 30-video plan template
1. **Videos 1–12 (~2 weeks, ~1 per day):** 3 formats × 4 videos. Each copies a series working **now** on new channels, with a fixed title template and a 1-second hook. No topic-hopping inside a format.
2. **Read at day 7 (our median is 1.6k):** signal = any video ≥10k or a format median ≥5k; drop = all 4 under 3.2k; breakout = ≥100k.
3. **Videos 13–24:** 8 in the winner (new topics, same template) and 4 in a new challenger.
4. **Videos 25–30:** make the winner a named series and refresh the hook as it decays. Nothing at 10k by video 24 → change niche or audience.
5. **Don't wait for the back catalog to recover.** Match length to the format (10–20s comedy, 20–40s rankings, 35–60s narration).
