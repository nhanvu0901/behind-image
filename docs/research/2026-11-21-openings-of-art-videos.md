## 1. The 15-Line Answer

1. **Presenters hurt reach in this sample:** 0 of 12 top-third videos (`>= 2.15M` views) use an on-camera host (`0%` vs `20.8%` in the bottom two-thirds), and faceless essays draw `1.25M` median views vs `388k` for presenter talks.
2. **Tension beats catalog speed:** Lower-view videos often recite title/painter/date faster (`10.4s` hard-fact median vs `16.0s` in top third), whereas `12/12` top-third videos deploy an active conflict hook (`contradiction`, `story`, `superlative`, `question`, `misunderstanding`, `mystery`) and establish stakes earlier (`15.4s` median / `17.1s` mean vs `18.9s` / `28.3s`).
3. **Earn the whole painting as a payoff:** `8/12` top-third videos (`66.7%`, median `16.0s`) hold back the full painting while starting on a tight detail, macro riddle, or visual puzzle, compared to `10/24` (`41.7%`, median `8.0s`) in the bottom two-thirds.
4. **Modern creator essays skip early idents:** While older uploads (2016–2018 Nerdwriter1) and institutional TED-Ed bumpers account for `6/12` top-third idents, `13/18` (`72.2%`) of 2020+ non-TED-Ed faceless videos start clean and omit or delay channel branding past `40s` (e.g., Great Art Explained at `52–84s`, Inspiraggio/Art Deco with none).
5. **YouTube's `0:30` benchmark demands hook-title match without repeating the title:** Creator and platform guidance (YouTube Analytics `Intro`, George Blackman, Ed Lawrence, `vox.style`) warns against greeting strangers, channel logos at `0:00`, or restating the title without a new question/paradox.
6. **Arnolfini head-to-head shows Raking Light's three bottlenecks:** Among 8 Arnolfini videos, top faceless hits enter speech in `0.16–2.40s` on tight detail crops, whereas Raking Light sits silent on the full painting (`s0000`) for `5.00s`, buries a tiny `3%` corner label gone at `5.0s`, and overlays `9.1s` of `ID-BRAND-03` typography upfront.
7. **Arnolfini also hides its core identity in speech:** Current `arnolfini-v3` `c01` (`149` words, `61.8s`, ends `66.8s`) never speaks *"The Arnolfini Portrait"*, names *"Jan van Eyck"* only inside the graffiti quote, and finishes Costanza Trenta's `1433` death collision at `44.0s` (after YouTube's `30s` Intro mark).
8. **Raking Light target opening (`0–60s`):** Trim silent lead to `1.5s` on the opening detail, speak work/painter/date outside quotes at `~9–13s`, pivot on *"but/yet"* before `30s`, land documentary proof (`~30–43s`), pull back to reveal the full panel at `~48–55s` with an open question, then place `ID-MARK-01`/chapter transition after `55s`.
9. **Proposed `arnolfini-v3` rewrite hits every target cleanly:** Same factual claims from `arnolfini_narration.md`, cut to `130` words (`53.9s` spoken, `55.4s` end), explicit outside-quote title/artist/`1434` by `~13s`, median sentence `14` words (`max 20`), `0` validator issues.
10. **Four reusable opening templates fit calm close-looking:** (1) Detail-to-Whole Paradox, (2) Macro Visual Riddle, (3) In-Media-Res Crisis, and (4) Misconception Reversal on Full Canvas.
11. **Enforce script openings in prompts + `validate.py`:** Require `b0001 <= 20–25` words anchored visually, outside-quote title + artist before `~72` words (`30s`), explicit conflict/question, and anti-roadmap regex checks (`"in this video"`, `"today we"`).
12. **Hand visual/timing edits to a dedicated worker:** Reducing `config/voice.json`'s `5.0s` lead, lowering `OPENING_MIN_FIRST_WORD_S = 3.3` in `bti/draft/zones.py`, shifting `ID-OPEN-01`/`ID-LABEL-01`/`ID-BRAND-03` in `style/gae/style.json`, and wiring visual cue timing belong outside the script stage.
13. **Title packaging separates hits from catalog entries:** Top-third titles are shorter (`49` median chars, `10/12 <= 60`), `5x` more likely to pose a question (`41.7%` vs `8.3%`), and usually drop artist names (`66.7%` omit) and channel suffixes (`91.7%` omit) while avoiding clickbait hype.

> [!WARNING]
> **Critical Confounders & Methodological Limits:** View counts are **not** controlled A/B experiments. High view counts in this corpus heavily reflect channel subscriber scale (TED-Ed, Vox, Nerdwriter1), upload age (`2014–2026`), subject fame (*Mona Lisa*, *The Starry Night*, *Girl with a Pearl Earring*), thumbnail/title CTR, and external school or museum embeds. Because external inspection lacks YouTube Studio's `0:30` retention curve, corpus differences capture shared norms of high-performing channels rather than proven causal effect size from the opening alone.

2. **Why `early_ident_0_15s` reads `6/12` in Top Third:** Disaggregating those 6 reveals a sharp split by era and institution: **3** are *TED-Ed* lessons (`2014–2022`, channel bumper `0–6 s` backed by >20M subscribers and school traffic), **2** are early *Nerdwriter1* uploads from `2016` and `2018` (`WKRKrpz09Fk`, `g15-lvmIrcg`), and **1** is *The Canvas* (`bvEHyeph1B4`, `2020`, which already speaks at `0.42 s` over its dark mark before later moving its ident to `40 s` in `osJPtHSUniA`). Across **post-2019 non-TED-Ed faceless uploads (`N = 18`)**, **`13 of 18` (`72.2%`) remove channel idents from `0–15 s` altogether** (either omitting them cleanly like *Inspiraggio* and *Art Deco*, or deferring branding past `40–84 s` like *Great Art Explained* and *Curious Muse*), and median `first_word_s` drops to **`2.92 s`**.

## 3. What YouTube and Working Creators Say About Openings (Part 2 Synthesis)

Across official YouTube Help docs, retention designers, strategists, and working essayists (`12` verified source groups cached in `.scratch/introresearch/web/`), five convergent principles govern the first `0–60 s`:

1. **YouTube measures `0:30` explicitly as "Intro":** YouTube Help notes that **Intro** literally *"tells you what percentage of your audience still watched your video after the first 30 seconds"* and succeeds when *(1) the first 30 s matched the viewer's expectation of title and thumbnail*, and *(2) kept the audience interested* without an early skip dip. Creator Insider's analytics designer ("Dan", Oct 2020) emphasized comparing `0:30` Intro retention against a channel's 10 latest videos of similar length to diagnose early abandonment.
2. **Never echo the title blandly or summarize what you'll cover ("False Start" & "Summary ≠ Hook"):** George Blackman identifies three retention killers—**Bland Start** (opening on a dull foundational platitude), **Too Much Too Soon** (resolving the premise upfront), and **False Start** (spending `10 s` repeating the title word-for-word, causing a skip spike around `0:10`). Every educational hook should anchor **Target**, **Transformation**, and **Stakes**. Ed Lawrence (*Film Booth*, `pdo42Yy0Fzk`) states flatly: *"a lot of people just jump in and they're like, I'm going to show you these three things... That's not a hook, that's just saying what you're going to say."* Instead: use a counterintuitive statement, a concrete question, a surprising fact, or drop *in media res* paired with matching visual hooks—remembering *"99.999% of them have never heard of you"* and *"viewers don't care about how smart you are, only how smart you make them feel."*
3. **Zero logo bumpers, immediate question/doubt:** `vox.style` (2026 codification of Vox explainers) instructs: *"Open with the viewer's doubt or question, not your brand"* and *"The first three seconds ask a question or name a doubt, without a logo."* Phil Edwards selects explainers around unexpected *Rosetta stone* puzzles tested against thumbnail + headline before greenlighting.
4. **Defer self-introduction, structure via *"Therefore / But"*, look first:** Tony Zhou (*Every Frame a Painting*, `1GXv2C7vwX0`) satirizes front-loaded introductions by moving his *"Hi, my name is Tony"* to the closing line, warning that *"video essays aren't essays. They're films"* connected by causality and contradiction (*therefore / but*) rather than *"and then"* lists. Evan Puschak (*Nerdwriter1*) distills the rule to *"be flat-out interesting"* through arresting sensory openings aligned word-to-image. In art education specifically, James Payne (*Great Art Explained*) removes *"art-speak"* to tell human context around one painting per film, while Beth Harris & Steven Zucker (*Smarthistory*) insist *"the object is as important as the text"*: inviting viewers into active *"close looking"* before abstract textbook framing. Alex Rosenthal (*TED-Ed*) echoes starting inside a concrete puzzle without *"square one"* prerequisites for early wins.
5. **Confirm thumbnail promise before minute one closes:** Paddy Galloway frames packaging as *"familiar, but unexpected"* (e.g., Veritasium's concrete black-balls hook), and the leaked MrBeast production guide observes that even blockbuster clicks lose ~`35%` in minute one (`21M / 60M` cited as *reasonably good*) unless minute one proves thumbnail expectations and hits a concrete proof-of-scale around `0:30`.

## 4. Second-by-Second Raking Light Opening Recipe + Variants

Raking Light's brand should remain calm, precise, and unhurried—never shouting *"THIS PAINTING IS INSANE"* or flashing YouTube comments. Calm voice does **not** require `5.0 s` of dead silence or hiding what painting we are inspecting.

### Recommended `0–60 s` Blueprint (Default)

| Window | Narration Role | Spoken Target | Visual / Camera Cue | On-Screen Text / Identity |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `0.0–1.5 s` | Visual breath (shortened from `5.0 s`) | Music / room tone only (`1.5 s` max lead) | Tight **detail crop** (or macro focal detail) slowly brightening (`ID-OPEN-01` trimmed `<= 1.5 s`) | None — let eyes register brushwork first |
| `1.5–9.0 s` | **Direct visual anchor** (`b0001`, `15–22 w`) | Imperative / direct look at the physical oddity onscreen; zero throat-clearing | Slow push or subtle pan within the opening detail | Optional light corner attribution (`x_pct=4, bottom_pct=92`) **only** after `2–3 s` if crop hides identity; disappears cleanly by `~8 s` |
| `9.0–18.0 s` | **Spoken anchor + first fact** (`b0002`, `22–30 w`) | Speak **painting title + painter's name + catalog date (where uncontested)** *outside quotation marks*, tied to what the crop shows | Second close detail or tight translation crop matching the claim | Clean quote/text callout **only if translating script**, never center-blocking title card |
| `18.0–30.0 s` | **"But / Yet" paradox turn BEFORE `0:30`** (`b0003`) | Break expectation before YouTube's `30 s` benchmark (`"Yet another date sits uneasily beside it"`) | Shift from opening detail toward contrasting figure/zone | None (`20–30 s` stays uncluttered) |
| `30.0–45.0 s` | **Documentary/visual proof (`b0004`)** | Concrete dated contradiction or technical observation establishing **stakes** (why the painting cannot mean what it first appears) | Guided close cuts on the specific clue/subject | Optional subtle year/name tag only if unfamiliar |
| `45.0–56.0 s` | **Earned wide payoff + unresolved question (`b0005`)** | Pull back across the composition and end `c01` on a sharp, unsettled question (`?`)—never previewing the rest of the script | **Slow pull back to full painting (`whole_painting_seen_s`)** as narrative payoff | None until after final word |
| `56.0–62.0 s` | **Post-hook breath & chapter/brand mark** | `~1.2 s` silence (`BREATH_S`) -> begin `c02` | Transition dissolve / dark breath | Subtle `ID-MARK-01` or muted chapter attribution (`c01 -> c02`), **never** front-loaded over `0–15 s` |

## 6. Script-Model Rules, Programmatic `validate.py` Checks, and "Needs Another Worker"

### A. Concrete Prompt Rules (`prompts/outline.md`, `prompts/script.md`, `prompts/rules_voice.md`)

1. **`prompts/outline.md` (Planning `c01` `ROLE: cold_open`):**
   - Require `h1..h3` for `c01` to specify: *(i) Concrete Visual Entry* (the specific detail, macro zone, or canvas paradox shown at `0:00`), *(ii) Contradiction Pivot (`but/yet`)* landing before `~30 s`, and *(iii) Unresolved Question* that stays open at `c01`'s end (`CARD: no`).
   - Explicitly prohibit summary outlines for `c01` (*"outline what this video will examine..."*).
2. **`prompts/script.md` (Drafting `c01` Beats):**
   - Keep `b0001` crisp (`<= 25` words, target `12–20` words) and anchored straight onto a visible detail or immediate concrete puzzle so the first spoken visual focus arrives inside `10 s`.
   - Require `c01` to speak **both the painting's title and painter's name outside quotation marks** (and year when documented/central) early inside `c01` (ideally beats `b0001–b0002`, within `~72` cumulative words / `~30 s` at `WPS_DEFAULT = 2.41`), rather than relying on on-screen labels or translated inscriptions.
   - Require an unresolved tension/paradox turn before cumulative `~72` words and an explicit closing question (`?`) at the end of `c01` that directly motivates `c02` without a filler *"Let's find out in this video"* roadmap.
   - Instruct `show:` fields on `c01` beats to sequence **detail -> contrasting detail -> wide pull-back** (unless `brief` explicitly flags a whole-canvas misconception hook).
3. **`prompts/rules_voice.md` (Voice & Hook Discipline):**
   - Add two explicit rules: *"Start inside an image or concrete human problem, never a channel greeting or agenda preview"* and *"Name the painting and artist in ordinary speech within the first ~25 seconds so listeners never wonder what work they are viewing."*
