**Copyright reality, 2026.** Three separate outcomes.
1. **Claimed.** A Short under 60s with a studio claim stays up. Non-music claims are "not credited" in the Shorts revenue split "even when a Content ID monetize policy is set", so the claim takes no share of our revenue ([YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12504220), fetched 2026-11-12). Because studios earn nothing from claimed Shorts, rights managers report a shift toward manual strikes and takedowns ([Tubefilter, 2026-01-07](https://www.tubefilter.com/2026/01/07/the-current-reality-of-rights-management-and-content-id-on-youtube)).
2. **Blocked.** Any Short over 1 minute with any active claim is blocked worldwide ([YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/13053317)). Hard rule: stay under 60s.
3. **Strike.** Manual takedowns hit anime hardest. In September 2026, Remove Your Media (which names Crunchyroll and Viz among its clients) filed more than a dozen strikes and removed 60+ videos from Nicholas Light's 1.3M-sub channel. YouTube later reversed them ([Dexerto, 2026-09-12](https://www.dexerto.com/youtube/anime-youtuber-says-channel-faced-deletion-after-copyright-strikes-removed-60-videos-3408486/)).
- **Monetization (separate from copyright).**
  - YouTube lists "unedited clips from others' movies or TV shows" as ineligible.
  - On 2026-10-01 YouTube said it will cut the reach of re-uploaded Shorts. Per Rene Ritchie, narration that only describes what is on screen does not count as added value ([PPC Land](https://ppc.land/re-uploaded-shorts-lose-reach-as-youtube-favours-original-clips/)). REPORTED.
  - The reused-content review covers the whole channel, and movie recaps are flagged as high risk ([Milx, 2026-05-29](https://milx.app/en/trends/what-channels-have-a-high-risk-of-demonetization-in-2026)).
- **Studio stance varies.** Prime Video and HBO pay clippers through Content Rewards at $0.50-3 CPM ([Creator Rep, 2026-08-03](https://phil.beehiiv.com/p/even-hbo-is-running-a-clip-farm-now)), so Amazon IPs look the least hostile. Disney/Toei/Crunchyroll are the strictest.
- **How channels survive:** under 60s, clips of a few seconds each, crop/reframe, and narration that adds facts the footage does not show.

## Verdict

**Best 3:**
1. A, screen-hype comic explainers.
2. B, release-window explainers (Doomsday in 5 weeks).
3. C, comic vs show, limited to Amazon IPs.

**Avoid:** D, E, F and G. They are saturated, use the most hostile rightsholders, or rely on narration that only describes what is on screen. F also needs someone to watch the episodes, which our LLM research cannot do.

**Mixing show clips into comic videos:** fine in small doses, bad as the main visual.
- The franchise drives the views (The Boys and Invincible win in our data).
- The adaptation format underperforms (24-31k medians).
- Clips add reused-content and strike exposure without adding reach.
- Rule: at most 1-2 clips of 3 seconds or less, used as the hook ("the show did X"), with comic panels for the payoff, under 60s.
