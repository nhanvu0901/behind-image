## The task template as it stands, and every change to the draft

`skills/title/TASK.template.md` is the draft with these changes and no other (checked with `diff` against `SKILL_DRAFT.md`):

1. **A paragraph under the intro names the three files the agent reads** (`inputs/narration.json`, `inputs/outline.json`, `inputs/painting.json`). The mechanics need it (the draft only names the narration). Rule 3 is protected in the same paragraph: a word that is only in the outline or the painting file is never used in a title.
2. **Rule 3 gets one sentence** (from the research below): a title true to the letter that leaves a false picture ("I Bought a Ferrari for $1", said over a toy car) is struck. It makes rule 3 stricter, not looser.
3. **Rule 6's list, rule 7's list, rule 8's numbers, section 3 step 3's numbers, step 5's `4`, section 5's counts and form list become placeholders** (`{{VAGUE_PHRASES}}`, `{{BORROWED_WORDS}}`, `{{BEST_MIN}}`, `{{BEST_MAX}}`, `{{MAX_CHARS}}`, `{{MAX_WORDS_TEXT}}`, `{{WRITE_AT_LEAST_TEXT}}`, `{{FORMS_AT_LEAST_TEXT}}`, `{{FIRST_CENTRAL_MIN}}`, `{{MIN_CANDIDATES_TEXT}}`, `{{MAX_CANDIDATES_TEXT}}`, `{{FORMS_LIST}}`) filled from `bti/title/rules.py`, so the template and the check cannot differ. `tests/title/test_title_rules.py` renders the template, looks for every phrase and number of `rules.py` in the text, and compares the rendered rules 6, 7 and 8 with the draft's own sentences.
4. **Section 5's last paragraph lists exactly what the check refuses and what it only warns about.** The draft's list (a title over 60 characters or nine words, a banned phrase of rules 6 and 7, a colon, an unknown line id, a reason that is not Vietnamese, fewer than five candidates, two of the same text) is kept, and the mechanical consequences of rule 8 and 3 are added: brackets, an emoji, a word in capitals, more than one question mark, a number in digits that none of the cited lines holds, more than eight candidates, a score outside 1 to 5, a file that is not shaped as shown, a second half made by a dash or a bar. Warnings: a length outside the best range, quotation marks (the check cannot know the words are written in the painting), a title not in Title Case, a first choice that is not the highest total or scores under 4 on `central`, a title none of whose words is in the lines it cites. A word of rule 7 passes when a line the candidate cites says it (the draft: "unless the narration itself says exactly that").

The rendered rules 6 to 8 (the text the agent reads) match the draft's text: `("The Hidden Meaning", "More Than Meets the Eye", ...)`, `No "shocking", "secret", ...`, `28 to 55 characters is best, 60 is the limit ... nine words at most`.

## What the sources say (web research: 4 searches and 4 page fetches, generic queries with no personal detail, about 15 minutes)

Checked:
- **YouTube's own policy** (`https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/2801973`, "Spam, deceptive practices and scams", item 9, malicious clickbait): titles, thumbnails or descriptions that lead the viewer to expect something the video does not deliver are against the policy; the page says nothing about capital letters or punctuation. This supports rule 1 and rule 3 (a promise the video must keep) and gave no number.
- **A correlational study of 18,080 channels** (`https://air.io/en/audience-growth/how-to-write-a-youtube-title-that-gets-clicked-research-across-11-niches`, the vendor says: correlation, titles not separated from thumbnails): the 30 to 50 character bucket does best in nearly every niche, 90 and more characters do worst; a question is the strongest positive pattern in Education; ALL CAPS is neutral or negative in Education; emoji lower credibility in Education; brackets help a little in tutorial niches (year tags). No figures for history or art, no word counts. Reading: the draft's best range (28 to 55) contains 30 to 50 and its limit (60) is far under 90; its bans (capitals, emoji) agree with the Education findings; I kept the draft's numbers and did not adopt brackets (the evidence is about "[2026]" tags of tutorials).
- **Two editorial guides** (`https://1of10.com/blog/the-definitive-guide-to-ethical-clickbait-how-to-lie-truthfully`, `https://www.descript.com/blog/article/the-youtubers-guide-to-the-curiosity-gap-how-to-keep-your-audiences-interest`): opinion, no published data. The concrete, checkable parts: the payoff must be delivered inside the video; the "loophole title" (true to the letter, false in impression) is the danger zone; a gap neither too wide nor too narrow; restate the hook early. The first and the last are already in the draft (rule 1, rule 6, section 3 step 6); the loophole is not, so it went into rule 3 (change 2 above).
- **Title-length pages of tool vendors**: the hard limit is 100 characters; the visible part is cut at 45 to 70 characters, the numbers differ by page.

Could not check: the exact cut-off on a phone (the pages disagree and are not YouTube's); YouTube's own help page on title length (not found); any data for art or history videos (none found); causality (every performance claim above is a correlation). I added no banned phrase of my own: no source lists exact phrases, and the coordinator's lists stay as they are.

## Decisions where the brief was silent

1. **A word of rule 7 passes when a cited line says it.** The draft allows "shocking", "the first" ... "unless the narration itself says exactly that and gives its reason"; the check cannot judge the reason, so it requires that a line the candidate cites holds the phrase (`rules.borrowed_in_text`). Rule 6's phrases never pass.
2. **Warnings are only printed**: `./check` prints `N error(s), M warning(s)`; only errors start the repair round (`Report.problems()` is None without errors).
3. **`title.generate` advances only while the project has no storyboard.** A title written after a storyboard exists applies the identity again and so ends the storyboard's approval; an automatic run must not do that by itself. With a storyboard the action stays listed and ready (asked by hand it works) but does not advance, and the state warns once (`title_missing`) when the storyboard has no title. The warning and the draft's wait are skipped when the saved storyboard already carries an `identity.video_title`.
4. **`draft.start` waits (need `title_done`) while the title is `none` or `stale`, the narration is approved and an agent could write it** (the title agent is usable). A project whose title agent cannot run does not wait.
5. **A title the agent wrote is `stale` when the narration revision now differs from the one it was written from** (the revision ignores the approval record, so approving the same text again keeps it fresh). A title set by a person or an operator is never stale. A generation asked for by hand replaces it; the old one goes to `history` (20 kept).
6. **Setting or generating a title while a storyboard exists applies the identity again in the same request** with the style the storyboard already has (the default style when it has none, as `PUT .../identity` with only a title does). The same title again changes nothing (the approval keeps standing). A refusal of the engine never undoes the title: the answer's `storyboard.error` and sentence say so. The job does the same through a hook (`bti.title.apply`) that `create_app` registers, because the job has no access to the app's save path.
7. **No separate approval and no gate.** The page and the command say "set" (a person or an operator).
8. **`title.set` takes `--set text=...`** (the generic action call; its template carries `base_rev`). Picking a candidate by number is `PUT {"base_rev", "option": n}` (`--body FILE`), or give the candidate's text.
9. **Limits of a session: 10 minutes, 40 steps, one repair round** (the brief says "about a minute of a model"). `title.generate` accepts `model` in its body like the other agent routes; without it the step's agent is used (the select of step 2).
10. **The Claude allow rules still list the pictures job's `./crop` forms** (`session.allow_rules()` is shared and I may not edit `bti/picks/**`): no `crop` file exists in the title's work folder, so they allow nothing. See "Needs another worker".
11. **Existing tests that the new role and actions change** (small edits, all listed here): `tests/state/test_state.py` (the exact action lists of the nine stages now hold `title.generate`, `title.set` and a waiting `draft.start`; its `fakes` fixture also stands in the title starter), `tests/auto/conftest.py`, `tests/cli/conftest.py`, `tests/evals/conftest.py` and `tests/picks/test_auto_api.py` (one line each: the agents file gives the title role an unknown model, so the title never advances in those worlds and their runs and next lines stay as they were; `tests/state` and `tests/title` have the mock agent for the title, so the title is really there), `tests/cli/sessions.py` (a fixture flag `title` switches the title agent on for the worked session of `skills/title/SKILL.md`, and the title's starter is a quick real job like the others) and `tests/cli/test_cli_docs.py` (`skills/title/SKILL.md` joins the replayed documents), `tests/output/test_api.py` (a storyboard with no title has its own warning: the assertion names the output folder's warnings only), `tests/settings/test_agents.py` and `test_settings.py` (the step `script` has three roles, there are seven roles). `bti/output/publish.py`: the "nothing changed" shortcut treats the `title` part of a project with no title as nothing to write (otherwise every project without a title would be rewritten at every change).

## The calls the coordinator makes on the real project `eligius`

After the branch is merged, deployed and the app restarted (new routes), with `bti` on the server (`tools\bti.bat`):

1. `bti status eligius` (it shows `title: none yet ...` and, since the project has a storyboard, the warning `title_missing`).
2. **One real generation, to see what the agent proposes:** `bti do eligius title.generate --yes-cost --wait` (the agent is the one chosen for step 2; to use Opus once without changing the step: `--set model="claude:claude-opus-5-5@high"`). Then read `GET /api/projects/eligius/title` (the candidates with their reasons). The first candidate becomes the title in use and, since a storyboard exists, is applied to it (its approval stops standing).
3. **The owner's title:** `bti do eligius title.set --set text="The Saint Who Was Also an Advertisement"`. It becomes the title in use (`source` `person`: never stale, never written over by the automatic run), is applied to the storyboard in the same call, and the agent's candidates stay listed on the page. Done in this order the owner's title is the one the video shows; in the other order the generation replaces it and it has to be set again.
4. Then the steps of `docs/qa/FIX_intro_flow.md` (the silences, `voice.generate`, `storyboard.retime`, the approval, the export); step 3 of that list (`PUT .../identity` with `video_title`) is replaced by item 3 here, and the retime applies the identity again with the title the storyboard carries.
